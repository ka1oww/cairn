# How a written line dresses — 25 August 2026

The last question left open by design round ten. [Round nine](2026-08-22-book-round-nine.md)
put authored words into the book and argued that the book can then tell the
truth in two registers: what the app derived, set in type; what a person
actually wrote, in their own hand. Turn 18's surface `18e` drew four ways a
written line could dress and priced each on the captain's board. It was
escalated rather than decided. This settles it.

## Register 3 — typeset, set apart — is the rule

Italic, full ink, the mount's own tilt. A written line is visibly a voice and
never claims a pen. It is the natural extension of `17d`, the blend the round-9
handoff recommended: treatment A warmed only by what the app already owns.

The set-apart part is the whole of it. A line that merely joined the derived
type would lose the distinction round nine bought — you could not tell what a
person said from what the app computed. Italic, full ink and the tilt of the
mount separate the voice without dressing it up as handwriting.

## Register 4 — the writer's own strokes — is an optional flourish

Finger-written at the capture breath, when the writer chooses it. It is never
required, and never the default: a line offered in register 4 and declined
falls back to register 3 and is not lesser for it.

This is the honest version of a handmade mark, and the reason it survives while
register 2 does not: the strokes are the writer's actual strokes, made by that
person's finger at the moment of capture. The book is recording labour that
happened, which is precisely the test round nine set.

## The rejected registers, and why they are kept here

**Register 1 — uniform typeset.** The written line is set exactly as the
derived line. Rejected: it throws away the distinction that made authored words
worth having. If the book cannot show that a person spoke, it may as well not
have listened.

**Register 2 — a script face.** The written line is set in a handwriting
typeface. Rejected explicitly, and it is the same lie round nine already killed:
simulated handwriting in a generated book records labour nobody performed. Round
nine could reject it in the abstract, because there was no author. Register 2 is
worse — it tells that lie *about a real person*, dressing what they genuinely
wrote in a hand that is not theirs. A font is not a person's writing, and the
book must not imply it is.

## The governing rule

**The book may only look handmade in ways that are true.** Register 3 does not
look handmade, so it may look however it likes. Register 4 looks handmade and is,
so it may. Register 2 looks handmade and is not, so it may not. That rule decided
all four, and it decides the next one.
