# The moment — 22 August 2026

The central decision in Cairn, and the one that reversed the original design.

## What changed

**The daily ping is no longer simultaneous.** Each person is pinged once a day
at their own time. The waking day is divided into as many slots as there are
people on the trip, one person per slot, reshuffled daily, so the pings are
spread across the whole day and never cluster. The day's photos consolidate
into one page at midnight, in time order.

**The end-of-day seal is silent.** No notification when the page closes.

## Why the simultaneous version failed

The original mechanic was BeReal's: everyone's phone buzzes at the same instant,
everyone takes a photo, the photos become one panel.

The objection that killed it, from the captain:

> What's unique about BeReal is that if everyone is with their own friend groups
> and then they get a BeReal, they can take one and everyone can see, oh, what's
> everyone doing? But on a trip you're always together. If four people get their
> phones buzzed at one time, then where do all four of them look? They all have
> their own phones. They all need to reply to the buzz. Wouldn't that defeat the
> whole purpose?

Two things follow, and both are correct.

**BeReal's value was information, and information requires distance.** You were
apart, so the photo told you something you did not know. On a trip everyone is
co-located and the information value is exactly zero.

**Worse, the mechanic damaged the thing it was meant to record.** As drawn, the
moment was a selfie. So the app's flagship mechanic produced photographs of
eight people staring at screens, taken at the precise instant they stopped
paying attention to each other — then bound them into a book.

## Why scattering solves it structurally

At 11:40 exactly one phone buzzes. That person is the only one interrupted, and
therefore the only one holding a phone. They look up and photograph the others.

**The camera turns around by itself.** No rule is needed, and no instruction has
to be given to eight people. An earlier proposal — keep the simultaneous buzz
but require each person to photograph someone else — solved the same problem by
force and was abandoned in favour of this.

Scattering also improves the record. One shared instant catches one second of a
day, which might be the coach ride. Eight scattered pings catch eight different
parts of it: breakfast, the walk, the queue, the meal, the night.

## What survives, and what does not

Cairn has five load-bearing properties. Scattering touches one.

| Property | Under scattering |
|---|---|
| Contribution gates access | Unchanged |
| One interruption per person per day, never otherwise | Unchanged — each person still gets exactly one ping |
| Photos nobody would have chosen to take | Unchanged, and this is the real differentiator |
| Each day produces exactly one artefact | Unchanged |
| The artefact is a shared **instant** | Becomes: the artefact is a shared **day** |

An earlier position held that scattering "removes the differentiator". That was
wrong and is corrected here. The differentiator was never simultaneity — it is
*unchosen photos*. A shared album holds the pictures someone decided were worth
taking; a pinged photo is the one nobody would ever have taken. Simultaneity was
only the delivery mechanism, and it was the mechanism that required distance.

What is genuinely lost is the moment the group notices itself — the small
"everyone's phone just went" beat. That is real, and it is the price.

## Why the seal is silent

A notification when the day's page closes was proposed, to recover that lost
beat, and then withdrawn. It does not survive scrutiny:

1. The page seals at midnight, the worst available hour on a holiday.
2. The content has no urgency and does not decay — it will be there in the
   morning, unchanged. Notifying about something that can wait indefinitely is
   the textbook bad notification.
3. It would not have restored the shared beat anyway. Half would look that
   night and half the next morning.

It would also have spent the single-interruption promise bought by cutting the
second ping.

The shared beat does not need the app. Someone at dinner says "the page is done"
and the phone goes round the table. Manufacturing that with a notification is
the app inserting itself into a moment that would have happened anyway.

## Consequences

- The four-up grid is retired on every surface. The day page is a timeline.
- A time-ordered timeline scales to any number of people with no grid, no empty
  seats, and no gap — which resolves the "what does the panel look like at
  eight people" question more cleanly than a variable grid.
- The book becomes one spread per day, photos in the order they happened.
- Dual capture returns in a different shape: the back camera is now primary,
  because the photographer is looking at other people.

## A note on the name

A cairn is a pile of stones, each placed by a different person passing through
at their own moment, accumulating into a marker. Eight people placing stones at
the same second is not a cairn — it is a ceremony. The scattered model is closer
to the name than the original design was.
