CAIRN · DESIGN ROUND 10 · 22 AUG 2026

The words, and the paper.

Two decisions landed on 22 August: the book generates itself and nobody edits it — it prints the words people had already written — and authored words are wanted, with the cover’s face settled as the photograph. The first decision quietly created a screen nobody had drawn: in the whole design there was nowhere to type a word. And the book itself had only ever been drawn as a phone reading a book — never as ink on paper a person can order. This round draws both missing surfaces. Governing rule throughout: a caption is the trip’s voice, not a form field — wordless is the comfortable one-tap default, and nothing ever asks.

Where a word is written: the breath, not a form

The record answers where before anything is invented: 10d — the confirmation breath between shutter and flip — is the one drawn moment where a person already holds their photo on a paper sheet. The caption is one dashed line on that sheet, anchored under the hour it will print beside. No new screen, no label, no field chrome.
