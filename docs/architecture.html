Cairn — the dependency map

Every piece of the app as a node; an arrow means “knows about” and always points downward. Point at any node and its inward arrows are what breaks if it changes. Drawn 22 Aug 2026 from main (PRs #7–#10 merged) and design round 7 (PR #11); updated the same day for the app scaffold, 24 Aug 2026 for the paste-and-confirm flow — the first real screens — Today, the day page that replaced its placeholder as the launch surface, and the Trail with the tab container; and 25 Aug 2026 twice, for the Pool (the third tab and the read seam under it) and for capture (the ping, the camera, the pause and the word, and the store that seam now reads). The greppable twin of this page is architecture.md.

The app has a way in, and a trip with all three of its destinations in it. The paste-and-confirm slice replaced the scaffold's proving screen and its disposable trip_drafts table — the sidestep in which a drafted trip carried no id at all, now closed at both ends: paste a plan, see what was understood with the doubt surfaced per cause, flip ambiguous dates in one tap, accept — and the itinerary persists into Drift, local-only for now. Today then replaced that slice's placeholder: the app opens on the day page for today's date — the day's identity, its stops in pasted order, a time only where the plan starred one. The Trail added the trip-level view — one node per day on the winding path, a flag on today — and the real container it and Today live in: a tab bar built so the Pool becomes a third entry, and shown without one until it existed. The Pool is now that third entry: the trip's photos grouped by day, newest first, over a read-only seam. There is one day screen and no separate day detail; the Trail opens that same widget for every node it draws. Capture then put a store behind that seam and gave the trip something to hold: the schedule is dealt for every dated day, the day you are standing in says where your moment stands, and an open or a late window leads to the shutter, one retake, one line of words, and a row in the photo index — which the Pool, reading the same store, draws. Back-only, which is the line's own scope. Two honest holes remain in it — nothing is registered with iOS, so no pocket buzzes; and where there is no camera the app draws its own frame, so a green Simulator run says nothing about the real camera path. The gate now agrees with the record: today's photographs stay shut to you until you have put something into today, and every day that is over is open to everyone who was there. It is one rule (GateState.decide), answered in the app in one place, and the Pool is the only surface drawing a photograph for it to withhold yet. The trip itself is stored now rather than implied: a roster, the fact of who started it, and three-word invite codes that die when the trip closes, with the permission model in cairn_model and a sheet off the Trail's title over it. The pings are dealt across that roster — which holds one person, because nothing carries a membership to another phone yet. lib/README.md spells these bands as directories. Every other designed screen, the import sweep, the remaining platform glue and the whole remote side are still intention, drawn here so the intention is inspectable.

Repositories — the seam

The waist of the hourglass: the only layer that knows the phone has a local database and that a network exists. Above it, nobody can tell where an answer came from. Everything awkward about offline-first lives here so it lives nowhere else:

photos out: read-only interface photos in: one Drift store outbox: bytes first, row second day_pages insert → update fallback deletions need refetch package dialects → vocabulary itinerary in and out shared facts reconcile: last write wins, per day photo sync & conflict policy undecided

Drift store — local SQLite

Typed tables, real joins, watchable queries — the streams that make the UI reactive. Native backend on iOS. Holds the itinerary tables (days, stops, set-aside lines), photos — the local index whose bytes sit beside it on disk, the same split as Postgres and R2 — and the trip: trip_facts, trip_members (no role column, ever) and trip_invite_codes (no expiry column; a code dies with its trip). Schema v6; the trip_drafts demo is dropped, v5 gives a trip written before the mint a real uuid, and v6 adds every day's merge clock plus sync_states — the cursor into the shared copy, and the one table here holding no shared fact. trip_facts.trip_id is minted here on the phone — the counterpart of trips.id's default gen_random_uuid(), moved so a trip can be started with no connection.

choice validated in the demo, built on, and recorded in docs/decisions/2026-08-25-riverpod-and-drift.md

Why the seam is the map’s centre

Every question a screen asks funnels down through app state into one layer, and that layer alone knows both storage backends exist. A provider asks “photos for day 4” and cannot tell — by construction — whether the answer came off disk or over the wire.

Skip it and every screen grows its own opinion about caching, retries and which copy of a photo is real: eight opinions, all subtly different, none testable in isolation. The seam is where the outbox ordering, the day_pages conflict fallback, deletion refetch and the translation of package dialects into the vocabulary live — so they live nowhere else.

Invariants the import graph won’t show

  1. “A day’s clock is fixed where it starts” is encoded three times — in cairn_model, trip_moments and photo_day_assignment — by siblings that cannot see each other. Change one, change all.
  2. The gate rule exists twice on purpose, and only twice: GateState.decide on the phone — answered by Trip.gateFor and by the app's day_gate.dart — and day_page_is_open in SQL for signing downloads. They must move together, and a third copy per surface is the thing to refuse in review. The rule: the gate applies to the day being lived; every sealed day is the party's.
  3. The trip clock has one source — the trips row. No phone ever infers it; two phones inferring differently is silent schedule drift.
  4. The ping derivation is frozen. Any change splits devices mid-trip; the only safe change is a loud namespace bump (v2 → v3). The trip id is one of its inputs, so the id is frozen too: the phone mints it and the server keeps it rather than reissuing. Renumbering a trip re-deals every remaining day of it, silently.
  5. Multiplication and addition, never bit-shifts — dart2js ints are 32-bit under shifts. Golden tests pin it.
  6. RLS refuses by filtering, not raising. Assert on table state afterwards, never on whether a statement threw.
  7. Membership checks only via is_trip_member/is_trip_starter; never force row level security — either revives infinite recursion.
  8. Where a trip stands is decided once — tripStandingAt in cairn_model, reached through tripStandingProvider; nothing above it compares dates itself. The close is enforced on both sides: seventy-two hours after the last day seals, written as graceAfterATrip and as trip_grace_after_end(), with the probe comparing the two, and shutting the pool in CaptureFlow.turnTheDayOver and in photos_insert_trip_member. Eight phones means one wrong clock. What it never takes is a person's hold on their own photograph.

Honesty about the gaps

Drawn as open, not settled. None is a contradiction: in each case the decision record is clear and the code is behind it, acknowledged and queued in docs/roadmap.md.

  • Starter & container decisions implemented on the phone, not on the server. cairn_model’s trip_powers.dart holds the whole model — rename and mint flat, delete gated once others’ photos exist, revoke by minter or starter, removal power passing to the longest-standing member — and the trip’s sheet asks it before every act. The migrations still have starter-only rename/delete and a max_uses the phone has no notion of, though the three-word code and its death at the trip’s close are reconciled now; the timezone power has nowhere to live until a trip clock is stored.
  • An invite code is real and cannot let anybody in. Minting, rotating, revoking and expiry-with-the-trip are built, and saying a code back is answered honestly for every case this phone can see. Joining needs a membership that reaches another phone — Phase 2.
  • The ping is dealt over a real roster that holds one person. The party is the trip’s stored members now, and the collision-free promise is exercised against eight through a seeded roster in the tests; a phone can still only write its own row.
  • Nothing is registered with iOS. The schedule reaches a NotificationEdge and stops. The whole ping path is real except its last inch — the one the user would notice.
  • No viewfinder, and a drawn frame where there is no camera. Capture takes a frame without showing the live preview, and falls back to a generated image on the Simulator. That makes the flow walkable without a cable; it also means a green Simulator run is no evidence the real camera path works.
  • No download path. Only uploads exist. The requirements are written down — which is exactly why the blank is the most dangerous one on the map.
  • Sync & conflict policy is settled for the itinerary and the roster — last write wins, per day, no conflict UI and no CRDT, which costs what last-write-wins always costs: a phone with a fast clock wins edits it should lose. For photos it is still undecided beyond three written notes, and nothing reconciles rows against R2 objects in either direction.
  • The shared roster replaces this phone's, and this phone is now whoever signed in. localMemberIdProvider is the account id when there is a session and the string 'me' when there is not; main() resolves it out of the session vault, with no network round trip, before the first frame — so nothing is credited to the stand-in and then re-credited — and _applyRoster runs for real on an ordinary build. The identity is fixed for the life of the launch. Two things this leaves open. A trip started under 'me' can never become a trips row (created_by references profiles.id, a uuid; _createSharedTrip is refused for the life of that trip) and nothing migrates it — which is the plane, and also a first launch on a merely slow network: a phone with nothing in its vault waits three seconds for an account and then runs as the stand-in, with no signal to the person that it happened. The budget is deliberate, so that is a gap recorded rather than solved. And the roster the server hands back names people by profiles.display_name, which nothing on the phone writes, so an anonymous account reads as New traveller rather than You. Both close when sign-in asks for a name.
  • One fixed clock per trip on the server (v1). First/last-day windows and the country-change rule live only on the phone. Grill round one §2 made the itinerary a synced shared fact and the schema now holds it, merged last-write-wins per day; that rule is written twice on purpose — in sync_trip_itinerary and in lib/repositories/itinerary_sync.dart — and never three times. The path is live as of 2026-08-26: an ordinary build points at the hosted project and signs in anonymously, and the one thing that proves it is test/hosted_smoke_test.dart — a green flutter test proves nothing about a hosted project, because every widget test binds NoSession.
  • The book’s interior is designed — round 9 drew it against the captain’s printed reference; the printed page is not designed, and the book is deliberately after the first release.
  • In-transit photos on a multi-zone day are parked — refusal (outsideTrip) plus manual placement is the current answer.
  • Derived variants, keepalive, CI: no server-side variant pipeline, and none is needed to draw a photo small — the phone decodes the stored original down to its box (lib/screens/photo_frame.dart); the between-trips dormancy keepalive is built and deployed as a Cloudflare Worker (ops/keepalive-worker/, documented in supabase/README.md’s Free-tier limits section); no CI runs any test on any PR.
  • The hosted Supabase project is real; a real R2 bucket still is not. All ten migrations are applied and an ordinary build points at it, but the adversarial RLS probe still runs against a throwaway local Postgres, and must.
